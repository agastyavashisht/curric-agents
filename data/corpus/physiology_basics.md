# Physiology Basics — Reference Corpus

## Cell Biology
The cell is the basic unit of life. Plasma membrane: phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins — controls what enters/exits (selective permeability). Nucleus: contains DNA, directs protein synthesis. Mitochondria: site of oxidative phosphorylation (ATP production via electron transport chain and ATP synthase). Endoplasmic reticulum: rough ER synthesizes proteins; smooth ER synthesizes lipids, detoxifies. Golgi apparatus: sorts, modifies, and packages proteins for secretion or intracellular use.

## Homeostasis
Homeostasis is the maintenance of a stable internal environment despite external changes. Negative feedback: a deviation from set-point triggers a response that reverses the deviation (e.g., blood glucose regulation by insulin/glucagon). Positive feedback: amplifies the initial stimulus, drives a process to completion (e.g., oxytocin during childbirth, blood clotting). Set point: the target value the body tries to maintain. Components: receptor, control center, effector.

## Nervous System
Neuron structure: dendrites (receive signals), soma (cell body), axon (transmit signals), myelin sheath (speeds conduction), nodes of Ranvier. Resting membrane potential: −70 mV (K+ leak channels, Na+/K+ ATPase pump). Action potential: depolarization to threshold (~−55 mV) → rapid Na+ influx → repolarization via K+ efflux → hyperpolarization → refractory period. Synapse: neurotransmitter released from pre-synaptic terminal binds post-synaptic receptors (EPSP or IPSP).

## Cardiovascular System
Heart: 4 chambers — right atrium/ventricle (pulmonary circuit), left atrium/ventricle (systemic circuit). Cardiac cycle: systole (contraction), diastole (relaxation). Stroke volume × heart rate = cardiac output. SA node (pacemaker) → AV node → bundle of His → Purkinje fibers. Heart rate: sympathetic increases it, parasympathetic (vagus) decreases it. Blood pressure = cardiac output × peripheral vascular resistance.

## Respiratory System
Ventilation mechanics: diaphragm contracts → thoracic volume increases → lungs expand → pressure decreases → air flows in (inspiration). Expiration is passive at rest. Gas exchange: O₂ diffuses from alveoli into blood (high to low PO₂); CO₂ diffuses from blood into alveoli. Oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve: sigmoid shape; Bohr effect — increased CO₂/decreased pH shifts curve right (reduces O₂ affinity, promotes O₂ release to tissues).

## ECG Basics
Electrocardiogram records the heart's electrical activity. Waves: P wave = atrial depolarization; QRS complex = ventricular depolarization (atrial repolarization hidden within); T wave = ventricular repolarization. PR interval = atrial depolarization + AV node conduction time. QRS duration = ventricular depolarization time (>120ms suggests bundle branch block). QT interval = electrical systole. Normal sinus rhythm: rate 60–100 bpm, regular P before each QRS.

## Muscular System
Skeletal muscle: striated, voluntary, multinucleated. Sliding filament model: myosin heads bind actin cross-bridges, pull actin filaments toward M-line, shortening the sarcomere. Requires Ca²+ (released from sarcoplasmic reticulum in response to action potential) and ATP (for cross-bridge cycling and Ca²+ re-uptake). Motor unit: one motor neuron + all muscle fibres it innervates. Recruitment: increasing force by activating more motor units (spatial summation) or firing faster (temporal summation).

## Endocrine System
Hormones are chemical messengers released into the bloodstream. Peptide hormones (e.g., insulin): water-soluble, bind cell surface receptors, activate second messengers (cAMP, IP₃). Steroid hormones (e.g., cortisol): lipid-soluble, cross membrane, bind intracellular nuclear receptors, alter gene transcription. Hypothalamic-pituitary axis: hypothalamus releases releasing hormones → anterior pituitary releases tropic hormones → target gland releases hormone → negative feedback to hypothalamus/pituitary.

## Renal System
Kidneys regulate fluid/electrolyte balance, acid-base, and excrete metabolic waste. Glomerular filtration: hydrostatic pressure drives plasma (minus proteins) into Bowman's capsule. Tubular reabsorption: 99% of filtrate reabsorbed (Na+, glucose, water, amino acids). Tubular secretion: K+, H+, drugs actively secreted. Countercurrent multiplier in loop of Henle creates osmotic gradient for water reabsorption. ADH (antidiuretic hormone) increases water reabsorption in collecting duct (aquaporin insertion). Aldosterone increases Na+ reabsorption/K+ secretion in distal tubule.

## Biosignal Origins
ECG: generated by cardiac ion currents (depolarization/repolarization waves). Electrodes detect potential differences on body surface. EMG (electromyography): sum of motor unit action potentials recorded by surface or needle electrodes. Amplitude and frequency reflect recruitment and firing rate. EEG (electroencephalography): sum of postsynaptic potentials from cortical pyramidal neurons. Rhythms: delta (0.5–4Hz, deep sleep), theta (4–8Hz), alpha (8–12Hz, relaxed, eyes closed), beta (12–30Hz, active thinking), gamma (>30Hz, cognitive processing).
